Tomodachi Life ROM: how to play it on PC, Mac and Android

Tomodachi Life never got a PC release, so playing it outside a Nintendo 3DS means emulation. This guide covers the whole chain — the emulator, getting a legal copy of the game file from your own cartridge, the settings that actually matter, and the bugs people hit most.

Reading time ~8 minutes · No downloads hosted on this site

Short answer

  1. Install Azahar, the maintained successor to Citra (Windows, macOS, Linux, Android).
  2. Dump your own Tomodachi Life cartridge to a .3ds or .cia file using GodMode9 on a modded 3DS. This is the only route we cover — downloading the game from a ROM site is copyright infringement.
  3. Open the file in Azahar, turn on hardware shaders and leave the resolution at 1×–2×.

On a modern laptop or a mid-range phone the game runs at full speed. It is a 2D-heavy title, so it is one of the kinder 3DS games to emulate.

Before you go further

Emulators are legal software. Game files are not free to pass around: downloading a Tomodachi Life ROM you did not pay for is piracy in essentially every country, and sites that host those files get taken down. This page explains how to create a backup from a cartridge you own, which is the defensible path. We host no game files and link to none.

What you need

An emulator

Azahar, free and open source. It replaced Citra after Citra shut down in 2024.

Your own game file

A .3ds, .cci or .cia dump of the cartridge or eShop copy you own.

A modded 3DS (once)

Needed only to make the dump. After that you never touch the console again.

You do not need a BIOS file. Unlike some consoles, a 3DS emulator runs most games without system firmware, though dumping your console's shared fonts and system archives fixes a handful of edge cases.

Setting it up, step by step

  1. Install Azahar

    Grab the current build from the project's official releases page. On Windows pick the .zip for your GPU generation and extract it anywhere — it needs no installer. On Android, install from Google Play or the GitHub APK; Android 10 or newer on a 64-bit device is required. macOS and Linux builds are published alongside.

    Avoid the ad-covered "Citra download" mirrors still floating around. Citra is discontinued; those pages serve old binaries at best and bundled adware at worst.

  2. Dump Tomodachi Life from your cartridge

    On a 3DS running custom firmware, with GodMode9 installed:

    1. Insert the Tomodachi Life cartridge and boot GodMode9 (hold Start at power-on).
    2. Open [C:] GAMECART.
    3. Select the .3ds file and choose Copy to 0:/gm9/out. A trimmed dump saves space; an untrimmed one is byte-identical to the cartridge.
    4. Power off, move the SD card to your computer, and copy the file out of /gm9/out/.

    If you bought the game on the eShop instead, dump the installed title to .cia from GodMode9's [A:] SYSNAND SD → title folder, or use a title manager.

  3. Point Azahar at the file

    First launch, Azahar asks for a games directory. Give it the folder holding your dump and the game appears in the list. Double-click to play. On Android use Add Games and pick the folder — keep it somewhere Android lets apps read, such as Documents, not an SD card root with restricted permissions.

  4. Set the controls you will actually use

    Tomodachi Life is almost entirely a touchscreen game. On desktop the mouse is the stylus, so you mostly need a key for A and B. On a phone it plays natively. A controller adds little here.

  5. Back up your save

    Your island lives in Azahar's save directory, keyed to the title ID. Copy that folder somewhere safe before any emulator upgrade. Islands represent dozens of hours and there is no cloud backup.

Transferring an existing island

You can bring a save from the real console across: dump the save with JKSM or Checkpoint on the 3DS, then drop it into the matching folder in Azahar's save data path. Match the region of the dump to the region of the game file or the game will reject it.

Best Azahar settings for Tomodachi Life

This game is light on 3D geometry and heavy on 2D sprites and audio, so the defaults are already close to right. Four settings are worth changing:

Recommended configuration — Azahar, desktop and Android
SettingValueWhy
Graphics APIVulkan (OpenGL on older Intel GPUs)Lower driver overhead; far better on Android.
Hardware shadersOn, with "accurate multiplication" offThe single biggest speed win. Accurate multiplication costs frames and fixes nothing in this game.
Internal resolution1× or 2×The art is low-resolution 2D. Above 2× you gain almost nothing and lose frames.
Frame limit100%Apartment and shop animations are timed to the frame rate; uncapping desynchronises audio.
LayoutLarge screen, small topNearly all interaction is on the bottom screen.
Audio stretchingOnPrevents crackle when the frame rate dips briefly.

Leave texture filtering and post-processing off. They smear the deliberately crisp pixel art and the Mii faces look wrong.

Common problems and fixes

Issues reported most often with Tomodachi Life under emulation
SymptomCause and fix
Black screen on boot, no errorUsually a decrypted-wrongly or incomplete dump. Re-dump the cartridge untrimmed. A .3ds from an unknown source is also the usual culprit.
"Encrypted ROM" or "Unable to find decryption keys"The dump is still encrypted. Dump via GodMode9's GAMECART option, which decrypts as it copies, or dump your console's keys and place them in Azahar's sysdata folder.
Mii faces render as flat colour blocksHardware shaders disabled, or a very old GPU driver. Enable hardware shaders and update the driver.
Microphone features do nothingMic input defaults to off. Set audio input to "Real device" and grant the OS microphone permission.
Camera-based Mii creation failsCamera emulation is incomplete. Build Miis by hand or import a QR code image instead.
Save file will not load after an updateSave paths changed between Citra and Azahar. Copy the old folder into the new save directory under the same title ID.
Stutter every few seconds on first playShader cache warming up. It settles after 10–15 minutes of play; keep the cache between sessions.
Game runs at 100% but feels slowThat is the game. Tomodachi Life is deliberately paced around real-time events. Nothing is broken.

Which Tomodachi game are you actually looking for?

Three different games share the name, and search results mix them up constantly. The ROM you want depends on which one you mean.

What Tomodachi Life actually is

It is a life simulator built around Miis. You populate an apartment block with versions of your friends, family and whoever else you feel like, then watch them form friendships, fall out, start relationships, marry, have children and develop oddly specific food preferences. You do not control them directly. You feed them, dress them, settle their arguments and hand out hats, and the comedy comes from how badly your characters behave when left alone.

The appeal is the voice synthesiser. Every Mii talks, with an adjustable pitch and speed, and hearing your own name read out in that flat synthetic drawl is most of the joke. It sold more than six million copies and remains one of the most-played 3DS games a decade on.

Frequently asked questions

Is downloading a Tomodachi Life ROM legal?

No. Tomodachi Life is still under copyright and still sold second-hand, and Nintendo has never authorised free distribution. Downloading a copy you did not buy is infringement regardless of whether you own the cartridge, in most jurisdictions. Creating your own backup from a cartridge you own is the defensible route, and it is the only one this site documents.

Are 3DS emulators like Azahar legal?

Yes. Emulators are clean-room software and courts have repeatedly found them lawful. What gets projects shut down is shipping copyrighted firmware or encouraging piracy — which is why Azahar ships no Nintendo code and asks you to supply your own game files.

Can I play Tomodachi Life on Android?

Yes, and it is one of the better experiences, because the game was designed for a touchscreen. Azahar's Android build handles it at full speed on most phones from the last five years. You need Android 10 or newer and a 64-bit device.

Do I need a BIOS or system files?

Not for this game. 3DS emulation works without system firmware for most titles. Dumping your console's shared font and system archives improves compatibility in a few games, but Tomodachi Life boots without them.

Why is Citra gone, and is Azahar safe to use?

Citra was discontinued in March 2024 when its parent organisation settled a lawsuit over the Switch emulator Yuzu. The code was open source, so the community continued it: Lime3DS and a maintained Citra fork merged into Azahar, which has shipped stable releases since March 2025 and is actively developed. Download it only from its official releases page — the old mirrors are not trustworthy.

Will my real 3DS save work in the emulator?

Yes. Dump it with JKSM or Checkpoint on the console and copy it into Azahar's save directory under the matching title ID. The game region of the save and of the dump must match.

Can I still use QR codes to share Miis?

Mii QR codes work if you feed the image to the emulator rather than a camera. Camera emulation is incomplete, so point Azahar's camera input at an image file containing the QR code. StreetPass does not work, since it depends on console hardware.

How much space and what hardware do I need?

Around 400 MB for the game plus a few hundred megabytes for the emulator and caches. Any CPU from roughly 2015 onward with a GPU supporting OpenGL 4.3 or Vulkan will hold full speed. This is not a demanding title.

Is there a Tomodachi Life PC version?

No official one. Nintendo has never ported the series to PC, and the 2026 sequel, Tomodachi Life: Living the Dream, is a Nintendo Switch exclusive. Emulation is the only way to play the 3DS game on a computer.

Our position on ROM files

This site is a guide, not a file host. We do not store, mirror, link to or help you find copyrighted game files, and we will not add them on request. The reason is partly legal and partly practical: ROM sites get DMCA notices, lose their domains and disappear, and the people who relied on them lose their guides too.

What we do document is the legitimate chain — owning the game, backing up your own cartridge, and running that backup in open-source software. If you do not own Tomodachi Life, physical copies are still widely available second-hand.
